I am a Ph.D. candidate in Computer Science at Princeton University, advised by Professor Jia Deng. I received my M.Eng. in Computer Science and B.Sc. in Computer Science and Mathematics from MIT, where I worked with Professors Joshua Tenenbaum and Jiajun Wu. Here is my CV.

Research Interests

I build controllable 3D worlds to help machines learn to perceive, reason about, and act in the physical world. My research connects procedural generation, computer vision, and robotics: how can we generate structured, diverse synthetic experience that supports generalization beyond the training data?

Through Infinigen and ProcFunc, I develop compositional tools for generating and editing objects, materials, and scenes. I use controlled scene changes to evaluate visual robustness, and I am interested in extending this control to synthetic data for robot learning. This direction builds on my work on visual concept learning and intuitive physics: learning reusable concepts and models of how the world works from limited experience.
